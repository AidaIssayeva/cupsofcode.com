What I do

I help Android organizations find and remove the bottlenecks slowing their engineers down.

That may mean long build times, unreliable CI, invisible retries, overloaded quality gates, unclear module ownership, or release processes that cannot absorb growing change volume.

My work combines engineering data, developer interviews, and hands-on technical experience to distinguish the constraint that matters from the dozens of optimizations that merely sound useful.

You can learn more about my Android Delivery Performance Audit .

My engineering journey

My Android career began in New York City in 2015, when I saved enough to attend a small hybrid bootcamp. After roughly two and a half months of coding 12–14 hours a day, seven days a week, I left the program early for a software engineering internship at Videri.

At Videri, I worked on long-running digital-display applications—including installations in Times Square. From there, I joined CLS and moved to the Washington, D.C. area to build a tablet dashboard that received vessel sensor data through a global satellite network.

Next came LiquidSky, where I helped build an Android app that let people access desktop games through the cloud. I later joined Clarity Money, a personal-finance startup acquired by Goldman Sachs, and continued building its mobile-banking product, Marcus.

I subsequently worked on Pickup at DoorDash, onboarding infrastructure and server-driven UI at Twitter, and Android developer productivity at Toast.

At Toast, from 2023 through April 2026, my work included build and release systems, CI reliability, merge workflows, code-coverage enforcement, and the broader developer experience around a large Android codebase.
